What is Le Guess Who?

Le Guess Who? is an international music festival in Utrecht, the Netherlands, held every November since 2007 (founded by Johan Gijsen and Bob van Heur). Over four days, about 150–180 artists play in venues all over the city centre, from concert halls to churches and clubs. The programme is adventurous and global: indie, folk, experimental, jazz, and music from cultures that rarely reach big stages. Each edition also has guest curators who pick their own favourite artists.

The festival is run by the non-profit Stichting Le Guess Who, which has ANBI status (a tax-recognised public benefit organisation) and therefore publishes its annual accounts. This page makes those accounts easy to read.

Artists with multiple shows

Policy goal: multiple performances per artist. In its multi-year plan 2025–2028 the foundation wants to work more intensively with part of the festival artists and ask them for several, preferably different, performances instead of one. The number of artists drops gradually from about 180 to 140 in 2028, while the total number of performances stays the same (more experiment and development, fewer flights, lower costs). Trivia

Future plans

The six goals of the foundation's multi-year plan 2025–2028, summarised from the official summary (PDF, Dutch). Mission: “Listening Is The Way Forward”.

Artistic

1. Deeper programming

No radical changes. Fewer artists, but more performances per artist: from about 180 acts a year to 140 in 2028, while the total number of performances stays the same. This also means fewer flights and lower costs.

Artistic

2. COSMOS becomes self-sufficient

The online/offline platform (films, radio, interviews, residencies) should run as an independent network after the 2023–2024 DigitALL grant, with costs shared with partners, Embassies and private income. LGW keeps funding two staff (project lead and producer); by 2028 COSMOS should run mostly on partnerships.

Artistic

3. U? and the city

The free city programme grows through local talent development and stronger links between U? and the festival.

Audience

4. Audience development, not growth

The festival is sold out and has no room to grow. 80% of visitors return. Focus points: the 20–30 age group, a better gender balance, and reflecting Utrecht's cultural and social diversity (one in three residents has a migration background).

Organisation

5. Sustainability and impact

New ecological targets every year (energy use, fewer international acts, sustainable transport, audience campaigns). Social safety continues with IVY Foundation, a code of conduct and a Social Safety Hub; accessibility work continues with Possibilize (project Superkracht).

Finance

6. Solid base, diversified income

Own income was 58% at the end of 2023, against expected cost increases of about 20%. Plans: make partner contributions measurable, grow private money (supporters programme, relationship manager, crowdfunding), find COSMOS partners, and raise ticket income (no more Early Bird tickets, higher service fees, more day tickets, night tickets and single concerts in Utrecht neighbourhoods).

COSMOS income ideas

COSMOS reaches about 37,000 people a year, but the annual reports show no COSMOS revenue. Rough, own estimates per year (not from the reports, based on typical industry figures and the 2025 reach). Realistic mix: about €100–250k per year, enough to cover much of the two staff LGW funds now, but not enough to be profitable without partners and grants.
